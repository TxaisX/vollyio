Finding 1 · blocks a shipment

Gemini is a broken judge as prompted

Median 97 on your real clips against the shipped engine's 65. Above your own labelled bands on 17 of 18 pro clips, eleven of them flat 100s. Never abstains, ever.

Consequence: the provider switch must not ship. It was never committed and the production key was never set, so nothing live was affected. The eval caught it first.

Finding 2 · a genuine win

Video works, and is 3x cheaper

$0.032 per run against $0.096 for frames, same 5s latency, comparable stability, quality difference of just +2.1 points. The modality was never the problem.

Consequence: video is the right direction once the judge is fixed. Token math confirms the provider samples about 1 fps, which is the open risk on contact timing.

Finding 3 · a live defect

Coverage is reported dishonestly

It counts checkpoints, not pointers. Median reported 100% against 80% actual. One analysis reported full coverage while judging 6 of 20 mechanics.

Consequence: the low-confidence flag never fires, so a thin read reaches a player as a complete one. Your own draft prompt already specified the fix.

Finding 4 · the deep one

A third of the rubric never worked

32% of verdicts unjudgeable, in four categories: needs audio, needs multiple reps, needs ball-spin resolution, needs a camera angle phones do not give.

Consequence: five pointers were a permanent invisible tax, heaviest on attack, your highest-volume and lowest-scoring skill. This triggered the full rebuild.

04What shipped into the working tree

Uncommitted. Gates green: policy lint, typecheck, 414 tests.

  • Scale-version plumbing. Scores and ratings now record which scale built them, and a rating re-seeds rather than blending across a recalibration. This is what makes any future scale change safe.
  • Two robustness fixes. A failed database read no longer wipes a player's rating history, and the eval route no longer parses every case file per request.
  • Eval infrastructure. A video arm, a concurrent resumable driver, a bridge that turns your stored clips into eval cases, and a score-band field in the labeller.
  • Documents. The rebuilt catalog with its six-skill verification addendum, the archived comparison run, and the decision-log entry recording why the curve constants were deferred.

05Recommended order

  1. Label eight clips, not thirty-six. The ones where the arms disagree most carry nearly all the information. Fifteen minutes, and it unblocks the rubric, the scale and the provider decision at once.
  2. Ship the two free fixes while you do: honest coverage reporting, and requiring a frame citation for every "I saw it" verdict.
  3. Fix weakest_metric, which currently makes every verdict read as a failure and hides real signal.
  4. Re-run the arms against your labels. Then, and only then, decide provider and modality with numbers instead of inference.
  5. Adopt the rubric and re-anchor the scale together, in one version bump, since both change every score and doing them separately costs two migrations.
The through-line of all of it: you are the only remaining source of ground truth. The engine cannot grade itself, and I cannot supply the labels without becoming a contestant judging my own contest, since the shipped engine is the same model family I am.

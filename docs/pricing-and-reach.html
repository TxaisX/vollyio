What it costs, what to charge, and how anyone finds us

Built from live production data pulled today: the Supabase telemetry rows, the Vercel analytics for the last 30 days, the org billing plans, and the shipped model layer. Every number below is tagged measured derived or assumed. Nothing here is copied forward from the July plan, because most of the July plan's arithmetic is now wrong.

The short version

Serving cost fell about 10× when the model moved to the gateway, so Pro at $9.99 now runs a ~94% margin and the entire business breaks even at roughly 8 paying subscribers. Pricing is therefore not a survival problem — it is a growth lever. The actual constraint is reach: 219 visitors in 30 days, and the single highest-yielding channel we have is a share link that nobody planned.

The one cost that grows without a ceiling

Storage has no retention policy

History is kept forever — docs/plan-matrix.md confirms no retention job exists anywhere in the repo — and every analysis stores the original clip at up to 100 MB plus 24 frames. Supabase Pro includes 100 GB. At a ~10 MB average that is about 10,000 stored analyses before overage begins at $0.021/GB stored and $0.09/GB egress.

Model spend scales with use and is trivial. Storage scales with cumulative history and never falls. At 10,000 accounts averaging 7 analyses each, storage is roughly 700 GB and becomes a larger line item than the AI. This is the only cost that can surprise us, and it is invisible until it isn't.

04The pricing question you actually asked

Weekly and annual plans are worth adopting, but for a reason that has nothing to do with cost recovery. At a 94% margin, price is not defending the business — it is choosing which customer we get and how much cash arrives up front. Here is what each billing cycle really nets.

Three things that table tells us

  1. Weekly extracts the most revenue per retained user and the most fee drag. $4.99/wk annualizes to $236 net against $113 for the current monthly plan — more than double. But Stripe's fixed 30 cents lands 52 times a year instead of 12, so 8.9% of the money evaporates versus 3.2% on annual. Weekly is a bet that the user churns before they notice the annualized price, which is precisely why VolleyVision's 10-SKU paywall drew resentment worth undercutting.
  2. Apple's cut hurts least exactly where weekly lives. At $99/yr, Stripe costs 3.2% and IAP costs 15% — a 12-point penalty. At $4.99/wk the gap narrows to 6 points. If we ship weekly pricing in the mobile apps, the store tax is at its most tolerable; if we push annual through IAP, we hand Apple $12 a head.
  3. Annual is the best deal for us and the honest one for the buyer. Lowest fee drag, cash up front, and it removes the churn machinery entirely. It is also the plan that already has a home: docs/billing.md §4.3 reserved the lookup key vollyio_pro_annual at $99 and never built it.

Do not ship a 10-SKU paywall

The competitive recon on VolleyVision recorded an "aggressive 10-SKU paywall ($4.99/wk to $59.99/yr)" and named paywall resentment as the thing to undercut. Adding weekly and annual takes us from 2 SKUs to 4. That is a ladder. Going further turns it into the thing we said we would beat, and at a 94% margin we have no financial reason to.

05The app store layer

Getting authorized on both stores changes the pricing math in ways worth deciding before the apps ship, not after.

  • Both stores charge 15%, not 30%, at our scale. Apple's Small Business Program covers developers under $1M in annual proceeds; Google Play applies 15% to auto-renewing subscriptions from day one with no enrollment. At our revenue this is simply the rate.
  • Apple requires IAP for digital subscriptions bought inside the app. A web-only checkout reachable from the app is what gets rejected under guideline 3.1.1.
  • The US external-link situation is real but unsettled. Following the Epic contempt ruling, US-storefront apps may currently show a web-checkout button and pay Apple nothing on those sales. The Ninth Circuit vacated the outright commission ban in December 2025 and remanded for the district court to set a fee "reasonably tied to Apple's actual costs." So the 0% is temporary and the number is unknown. It is also US-only: the same code path is a rejectable violation in most other storefronts.
The practical read: ship IAP because it is required, offer web checkout alongside it where permitted, and set expectations at 15%. Do not build a business case on the external-link loophole — it has a court date. And note that an existing subscriber who signed up on the web keeps paying through Stripe; the store tax only applies to purchases made through the store.

What the stores mean for the price itself

At 15%, $9.99 nets $8.49 against $9.40 on the web — a 91 cent difference on a plan that costs 58 cents to serve. It is affordable. The question is whether to price the same on both surfaces or mark up in-app to recover the cut. Price the same. Two different prices for the same product is the thing users screenshot, and 91 cents does not justify it.

The finding that should set the strategy

One share link

A single /share/… URL drew 74 visitors — a third of all traffic in 30 days, and more than the homepage's 113 relative to the effort behind it. The July plan's bet that "the analyzed clip is the ad" is no longer a thesis. It is the only channel with measured yield.

Everything follows from that. The share card is currently a link; the July plan already specified upgrading it to a video export — the clip with the score, the one-line fix, and a wordmark — and that remains the highest-leverage unbuilt thing in the product. A share link that yields 74 visitors as a static page yields more as something a teenager posts to TikTok without editing.

Channels, in order of evidence

  1. Shareable analyzed clips. 74 visitors from one link, zero cost. Build the video export. This is the whole strategy until something outperforms it.
  2. Reddit. 32 visitors from organic posting, unpaid. r/volleyball is 292K members. It is already working at small scale and costs nothing but time.
  3. Coach seeding and creator partnerships. Unproven for us. The July plan named specific creators at 100–400K followers and recommended rev-share over flat sponsorship. Correct order — but do this after the homepage converts, or we pay for traffic that leaks at 88%.
  4. App store listings. New, in progress. Worth having; nobody in the comparable set won on ASO alone.
  5. Paid ads. Not yet. At 219 visitors we have no conversion baseline to optimize against, so spend would buy the lesson we can learn for free.

07What has to be corrected before any of this goes public

The differentiator in the July plan is no longer true

That plan's central marketing claim was "Not an AI guessing from pixels. Real biomechanics, measured on your phone", resting on an on-device pose engine and measured per-checkpoint values. That engine is gone. There are no pose, ONNX, MediaPipe, or ML dependencies left in package.json — the runtime dependency list is Supabase, Next, React, Resend and Zod. Analysis is now a vision-model read of the video with holistic checkpoint observations and no per-checkpoint numbers, deliberately (D-099).

By its own definition the product is now closer to what that plan called the competitors' "VLM narrative" pattern. Shipping the old line would be a false claim about how the product works. It has to be rewritten before any campaign, landing page, or store listing uses it.

The good news buried in that

Phase 0 of the July plan listed an engine license swap as a blocking gate: the shipped rtm-body7 weights carried non-commercial training data and gated the ability to charge at all. Removing the engine removed the blocker. That gate is closed by deletion.

What we can honestly claim instead

  • Breadth. Six skills, indoor and outdoor. Every direct competitor found in recon does one skill or narrates generically; VolleyIQ was hitting-only.
  • It remembers you. Ratings, XP, goals, and the journey system that lets the coach answer "am I getting better at attacking?" from your own analysis history. This is genuinely hard to copy and nobody in the field has it.
  • It admits what it cannot see. The abstain lane, no per-checkpoint scores, no fabricated point deltas. A competitor's app that scores every rep 97 is the failure mode we measured and rejected (D-094). "It will tell you when the camera angle wasn't good enough" is a real, differentiated, defensible promise.
  • Ranked feedback, not a number salad. Three strengths and two changes, ordered — because ordering is what survived re-anchoring while absolute scores moved.

09Open decisions — these are yours

Annual at $79 or $99?

$79 reads as "under $7/month" and discounts 34%. $99 is already reserved in Stripe and matches the July anchor. I lean $79 — at a 94% margin the extra $20 buys less than the conversion it costs.

Is the week pass renewing or one-shot?

Auto-renewing weekly is worth more per user and is the model the direct competitor uses. Non-renewing is the version consistent with everything else recorded in the decision log about not shipping dark patterns. I lean non-renewing, and I think it is a marketing asset: "a week pass that actually ends."

Does free stay at 1/month?

It was set to 1 when an analysis cost $0.234 and the free tier was a real liability. At $0.024 the same generosity costs a tenth as much. Raising free to 2 or 3 a month is now nearly free and directly attacks the funnel leak. This is the pricing change with the best expected value, and it is a one-line constant plus a migration.

Do we turn on bot protection before marketing?

docs/billing.md §6 recommended it before any public push, on the reasoning that a monthly-resetting free tier plus open signup is a metered coaching API. The exposure is 10× smaller than when that was written — 500 farmed accounts is now ~$12/month, not $285 — but the recommendation stands and the work is already scoped.

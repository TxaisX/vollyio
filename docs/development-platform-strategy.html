Strategy · vollyio · 29 July 2026

From a scorecard to a practice habit

Turning vollyio from video-analysis software into a development platform is the right call. The hard part is not the nine pillars. It is that the promise underneath all of them — here is what to work on next — is the single least-validated output in the current system, and the people being promised it are minors.

Orientation

The thesis, and the three things that fight it

The repositioning is correct. Nobody has ever wanted analytics; they want to be better by August. But three measured facts in the repo constrain how fast this can be built, and every recommendation below is shaped by them.

1. The new promise rests on the one number nobody has checked

"What should I work on next?" is, mechanically, the weakest_metric field — the limiting fault the model names in each analysis. In the eval harness that field is labelled on 0 of 18 active cases, so the check that would test it has never executed once. Not failed. Never ran.

The rest of the baseline is not reassuring either: 2 of 18 cases pass overall, the score lands inside its expected band 7 times in 18, and every case in the suite is professional footage while the product sells to 13-to-18-year-olds. Zero cases at the target population.

Every pillar in the brief stacks a stronger claim on top of a score that has never been validated against the people paying for it.

This does not mean stop. It means order the pillars by how much unvalidated model judgment each one requires. Authored content (drills, learning, nutrition education) carries almost none. A weekly plan carries some. "NCAA D2 Readiness: 72%" carries all of it, which is why it is sequenced last and reshaped on arrival.

2. Nine pillars, zero users, and a February deadline

There is no external user. Not a low number — the auth.users table has one row and it is yours. Nineteen analyses exist, all yours, none since 23 July. A nine-pillar platform built before user number one is how six months of runway disappears into a product nobody has yet asked for.

The roadmap below is therefore ordered by what can be tested against ten to twenty-five real players you can recruit in person, not by what makes the feature grid look complete.

3. The users are children

Thirteen to eighteen is the difference between a product decision and a liability decision. Nutrition and strength pillars aimed at adolescents are not the same product as the same pillars aimed at adults, and the difference is not a disclaimer. This gets its own section at the end because it changes what those two pillars are allowed to be.

Already true, and better than the brief assumes

Much of the habit machinery exists: XP with a quadratic level curve, streaks pinned to Pacific with correct daylight-saving arithmetic, a daily challenge, 30 drills, six learning tracks, goals, a scoreboard, and a progression view that refuses to claim a trend without four reps and two days of movement. The engine is built. What is missing is that the daily challenge is a button that awards 75 XP for being pressed.

Correction to the brief

Coach chat is listed as a current feature. It is dark in production — gated behind COACH_ENABLED, which is off, with the route returning 404. Pillar 1 is the centrepiece of the new positioning and its surface is currently disabled. That is a build item, not an existing asset.

Deliverable 1

Product roadmap

Four phases, each with an exit gate that must clear before the next begins. The gates are the point. Without them this becomes nine half-built pillars.

Phase 0Weeks 1–2

Make the daily loop real and honest

Zero new AI cost. Everything here runs off content and data already in the repo.

  • Real daily challenge. Replace the honour-system button with a specific drill pulled from content/drills.ts, selected by the player's weakest skill rating, requiring a concrete input (reps completed, a self-rated outcome) before XP is awarded.
  • Analyses end on one action. The scorecard becomes evidence; the product is the single next step and the drill attached to it. No new generation required — all 19 stored analyses already carry priority_fix and drill_slugs. This is a reordering of the page.
  • Move XP awards server-side. xp_events is client-writable. That was vanity while XP was decorative; it stops being vanity the moment badges, milestones and the daily loop hang off it.
  • Weekly plan v1. Generated once per week from stored skill_ratings plus the last analysis, written to the database. Never regenerated on page view.
  • Recruit the cohort. Ten to twenty-five players from the Sacramento circle, onboarded in person, courtside, on their first upload.

Exit gate: ten players onboarded and the daily loop is usable by someone who cannot get to a court today.

Phase 1Weeks 3–8

Authored depth, and the first honest retention number

Every item is authored content with a rules-based selector. Marginal token cost is approximately zero, and authored content is also the liability-safe form for the nutrition and training pillars.

  • Learning Hub by position. Setter tempo and decision-making, libero serve receive and defensive positioning, outside approach and shot selection, middle blocking and transition. Ordered as a path, not a browsable library.
  • Training modules. Mobility, jump mechanics, injury prevention. Written as education with named sourcing, not as individualised prescription.
  • Nutrition modules. Match day, tournament day, hydration, recovery. Same constraint, harder: see the final section.
  • Parent digest. A fortnightly email showing what the athlete did and where they moved. Costs nothing, and the parent is the payer.

Exit gate: roughly 40% of the cohort films a second session within two weeks of their first, unprompted. This is the charge trigger from the original validation plan, and it is the only number that matters in this phase.

Phase 2Months 3–4

The parent's reason to pay, and a distribution experiment

  • Recruiting readiness as a checklist, not a percentage. Reshaped in the recruiting section below.
  • Highlight film assembly from clips already uploaded. The highest willingness-to-pay item in the whole product.
  • Club pilot. A free coach dashboard for one club director. Not a team product — a channel that reaches forty athletes in one conversation.
  • Eval repair. Capture and label intermediate and expert cases so the constraint-diagnosis check runs for the first time.

Exit gate: the weakest_metric check executes against target-population footage and clears a stated bar. Nothing in Phase 3 ships before this.

Phase 3Months 5–6

The claims that need the evidence to already exist

  • Position ratings and an overall rating, once per-skill scores are validated at the target level.
  • Milestones and improvement badges, awarded on measured movement rather than activity.
  • AI coach with real memory: seeded from stored ratings and plan history rather than by re-sending past analyses.
  • Recruiting readiness scoring, if and only if the underlying scores earned it in Phase 2.

Exit gate: paying subscribers who renew a second month at a rate you would be willing to publish.

Token discipline, since you asked for it explicitly

The measured analysis is 21,253 input and 2,257 output tokens, 44 seconds, $0.206. That is the expensive surface, and the platform expansion must not multiply it.

  • Generate the weekly plan once per week and store it. A plan regenerated on page view is the single fastest way to turn a $0.21 product into a $3 one.
  • Nutrition, training, learning and drills are authored files, selected by rules from stored ratings. Zero marginal tokens, and — not coincidentally — the only defensible form for advice to minors.
  • Seed the coach from stored state, not from re-sent analyses. The ratings table already holds the summary.
  • Cache TTL. Only 1 of 11 measured runs ever read from cache; the 7k-token system prompt is written at a 1.25x premium and expires unread on a five-minute window. Irrelevant at current volume, worth switching to the one-hour TTL the moment usage clusters.

Deliverable 3

UX redesign

One structural move carries most of the repositioning: the app must stop opening on a camera.

Duolingo does not open on a microphone. It opens on your path, with today's thing already chosen. vollyio currently opens on the most expensive, highest-friction action in the product — one that needs a court, a tripod, a partner, and good light. That is the analytics-tool posture expressed as navigation.

SurfaceTodayAfter
Home Dashboard of scores and recent analyses Today. Streak, the one assigned action, this week's plan progress. Scores move below the fold.
Analyze Primary navigation, the implied main action A contextual call to action: "Film this week's focus." Still one tap, no longer the front door.
Analysis result Scorecard with metrics and timestamps Headline is the limiting fault and the drill that fixes it. Metrics become the supporting evidence, collapsed by default.
Progress Charts for the athlete The parent-shareable artefact. Same honesty rules, plus an export.
Drills A library of 30 A path per position, with the next one already surfaced.
Keep this

The progression view's refusal to claim a trend without four reps, two days, and three points of movement is the most trustworthy thing in the product. As pillars are added, apply that same standard to every new claim. A platform that tells a teenager the truth about a plateau is worth more than one that manufactures a rising line, and it is the only defensible answer when a parent asks whether the number means anything.

Deliverable 4

Athlete engagement systems

The engine exists. It needs content behind the button and a reason to open the app on a day with no volleyball in it.

Fix first

completeChallenge() awards 75 XP for a click. No content, no answer, no evidence. Meanwhile xp_events is client-writable and row-scoped, so the level number is unearned twice over. This sits badly beside a progression chart engineered to refuse unsupported claims — the same product currently holds two opposite standards of proof, and the generous one is attached to the number athletes actually show people.

What the daily loop should be

  1. A specific assignment, chosen by rule from the drill library against the player's weakest rated skill.
  2. A required input — reps done, a self-rating, a one-line note. Small, but it makes the XP mean something.
  3. Court-optional alternatives on days the athlete cannot train: a film-study question, a mobility routine, a learning module. Roughly half of a teenager's days have no gym in them, and a streak that only survives on court days is a streak designed to break.
  4. Weekly reconciliation: the plan says five things; Sunday shows which happened.

Progression that stays honest

Keep XP for activity, because activity is what a habit needs. Keep ratings for ability, and never let one convert into the other. Badges should award measured movement ("toss consistency improved across five reps") rather than participation ("filmed ten clips"). An athlete who cannot tell which is which will stop trusting both.

Deliverable 6

Revenue expansion

On the proposed price change

The brief proposes $9.99 for 20 analyses. Live today is $14.99 for 18. That change cuts gross margin per subscriber roughly in half while raising the ceiling on cost.

Scenario Price Cap COGS at cap Stripe Net margin
Live today $14.99 18 $3.71 $0.73 $10.55 · 70%
Proposed $9.99 20 $4.12 $0.59 $5.28 · 53%

Recommendation: hold $14.99. You have no customers, which means no price resistance data — cutting price now optimises a variable you have never observed. A parent weighing this against a private lesson at $60 to $100 an hour is not deciding on five dollars. And realistic usage will be two to five analyses a month, not twenty, so the higher cap mostly buys the appearance of generosity while raising your worst-case exposure in exactly the month something goes right.

Add the annual plan, but at $149/year rather than $89.99. The annual's job here is cash today against a February deadline, and $89.99 sets a $7.50 monthly anchor that will be very hard to climb back from. If an entry price is needed, add a cheaper tier rather than discounting the flagship.

Expansion, in order of expected value

  1. Annual plan. Cash now, and annual subscribers churn far less by construction.
  2. Recruiting film and profile. One-time $49–99, or a Pro tier above. This is the item parents are most willing to pay for, and it requires no new model claims.
  3. Club tier. Priced per athlete per season. The revenue matters less than the distribution: one director conversation reaches forty athletes.
  4. Camp and clinic partnerships. A clinic hands out codes; vollyio becomes the homework after the weekend. This is how a product with no ad budget gets its first hundred users.

Deliverable 7

Recruiting readiness, reshaped

This is the strongest wedge with parents and the most dangerous thing in the brief. Both for the same reason: it is a numeric claim about a child's future.

Do not ship as specified

"NCAA D2 Outside Hitter — Readiness 72%" is a prediction about a teenager's college prospects, derived from a skill score that has never been validated against a single intermediate or expert athlete. If a sixteen-year-old shows that number to a parent and it is wrong in either direction — false hope or a child talked out of a sport — that is the screenshot that defines the product. There is no version of the eval suite that makes this defensible in the next six months.

Ship this instead

A readiness checklist built from observable, verifiable facts. It is more useful than a percentage, it is actionable this week, and every line is defensible.

  • Verified measurables. Approach touch, block touch, standing reach — measured from film. This is the one thing the vision model can establish with genuine confidence, unlike "shot selection."
  • Film completeness. Does the athlete have three to five minutes of clean, tagged, current footage? Most do not, and this is the item vollyio uniquely fixes.
  • Academic eligibility. NCAA Eligibility Center registration, GPA, core-course tracking. Nothing to do with volleyball, and a top-three reason recruits fall out.
  • Exposure. Which qualifiers and camps are on the calendar.
  • Contact activity. Coaches emailed, replies received, follow-ups due.

Show completion against that list. Say plainly that it measures preparation, not the likelihood of an offer. A parent can act on every line of it on a Tuesday evening, which is more than a percentage has ever given anyone.

Deliverable 8

Competitive differentiation

Hudl and its volleyball properties own team film: what happened in the match, for the coaching staff. That is a different job. Nobody in that stack answers "what should this one player do on Tuesday." The gap is real.

The competitive threat is not Hudl. It is that the athlete already has a club coach and does not perceive a gap at all. The differentiation has to be against the status quo of having a coach, and there the honest pitch is narrow and strong: a club coach sees twelve players twice a week and cannot review your toss frame by frame or remember what you were working on in March. vollyio does both. It is not a replacement, and positioning it as one will lose to the coach every time.

The unresolved strategic question

Your own advisory review found a competitor that reached 125,000 athletes through 12,000 teams and exited to Hudl, while a near-identical direct-to-athlete product reached roughly one to three thousand installs in five months. That is the strongest available evidence that the buying unit is the team, and it sits against the 29 July decision to target individual players.

The reconciliation worth testing in Phase 2: the athlete is the user, the club is the channel. Sell to the individual, acquire through the director. That keeps the product you want to build while borrowing the distribution that actually worked.

What is genuinely defensible

  1. Longitudinal memory. "The coach that knows your history" is real only if the history is stored, structured, and referenced. Two seasons of a player's rated reps is a moat a new entrant cannot copy on day one.
  2. Volleyball-specific rubrics. Six skills, per-discipline anchors, weighted checkpoints. Generic sports-AI cannot express this and generic AI wrappers will not build it.
  3. Calibrated honesty. Everyone else's demo shows a number going up. A product that says "this is flat, and here is why" is differentiated in a market full of flattery — and it is the only version a parent trusts twice.

Constraint

The minors problem, and what it changes

Two of the seven pillars are aimed at children's bodies. That is not a reason to drop them. It is a reason to build them as something other than personalised prescription.

Nutrition

Individualised calorie and macro targets delivered to a thirteen-to-eighteen-year-old athlete by software intersect directly with disordered eating risk, which is elevated in volleyball and elevated again in the recruiting-pressure population this product targets. The safe and still-valuable form is education, not prescription: what to eat before a tournament, how to hydrate across a three-match day, why recovery timing matters. Named authorship, no individual targets, no supplements at any point, and a visible route to a registered dietitian for anything individual.

Strength and plyometrics

Unsupervised jump-training prescriptions to a thirteen-year-old carry real injury exposure, and skeletally immature athletes are not a scaled-down version of adults. The same resolution applies: publish structured, progressive, authored routines with explicit form standards and volume ceilings, framed as education, with a clear statement that a coach or physio owns individual programming.

Consent and the payer

A guardian attestation checkbox exists on the upgrade path. That is a start and not a shield. If minors are the stated primary user, the parent should be a first-class account holder rather than an implied one — visible progress, control of the subscription, and a real record of consent. This is also, conveniently, the same build that makes the parent digest and the renewal case work.

Still open from earlier work

The payment provider's public support address is a home address and the support number is a personal mobile, on a product now explicitly marketed to children and their parents. That should be resolved before any acquisition push, not after.

In one line

What to do on Monday

Give the daily challenge real content, put position in onboarding, make every analysis end on one action, and get ten real players using it. Everything else in this document is downstream of whether those ten come back without being asked.

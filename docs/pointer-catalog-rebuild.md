# Pointer catalog rebuild

Status: PROPOSAL. Nothing here is wired into `lib/ai/pointers.ts` yet.

Six skills rebuilt from volleyball biomechanics and coaching-education sources, then
put through an adversarial observability review. Every pointer below is constrained to
what up to 64 stills of a single rep, shot on a phone from an arbitrary sideline angle,
can actually show. This replaces a catalog in which 32 percent of all verdicts came back
`not_visible` and five pointers had never once been marked `met`.

## Shape

Total: 120 pointers. Expected visibility across all of them: 46 high, 72 medium, 2 low.

## Serving (`serve`)

| Checkpoint | Weight | Phase | Why this weight |
|---|---:|---|---|
| `toss_quality` - Set-up and toss | 14 | Set-up through the highest point of the toss, plus the load frame for the non-hitting arm. Serve family is decided once here and reused: JUMP if both feet are off the ground at the contact frame, STANDING if either foot is still touching. That test is independent of any displacement measured anywhere in this catalog. | Cut from 18 as the reviewer asked, because the checkpoint carries coaching consensus only and two of its four slots were structurally unsound: the two axis-split toss pointers were mutually exclusive by camera position (each was not_visible whenever its sibling was visible), and the release-instant elbow angle sat in the 6fps context region on the far arm. Both are fixed at the source rather than papered over: the axis pair is collapsed into one azimuth-agnostic read that always scores, and the release read is now a position test on the largest available landmark. The freed slot goes to a load-frame read of the tossing arm, a different frame and a different fault. The toss also stops charging for the contact instant: its read is restricted to the apex frame, so contact_in_front owns contact alone. |
| `approach_and_jump` - Approach, drive and jump | 24 | Final approach step and takeoff through the airborne phase (jump family), or the step-and-transfer through contact (standing family). Family: JUMP if both feet are off the ground at the contact frame, STANDING if either foot is still touching. | Raised rather than cut, because the reviewer's objection was that 22 points evaporated on a standing serve, and that is fixed at the source instead of by moving weight: all four pointers now score in both serve families, and family is decided by a displacement-independent test (both feet off the ground at contact), which removes the circularity that made forward_drive unfalsifiable. The evidence here is the strongest measured in the skill (horizontal COM velocity at takeoff correlates with ball velocity r=.55, vertical with contact height r=.70), and every pointer is a whole-body displacement or a large-segment angle scaled by the athlete's own torso. lands_inside_endline is gone: it measured the same forward travel as its sibling, and its primary reference, the painted endline under oblique handheld perspective, does not exist on sand at all. Its share goes to the two halves of the plant, which are now separate single-condition pointers with contiguous bands. |
| `arm_swing` - Arm load and swing | 25 | The load frame — greatest hitting-elbow flexion in the six frames before contact — through acceleration into contact. | Raised from 18 and now the second-heaviest block, which is where the reviewer said displaced weight belongs: these are the most projection-tolerant reads in the catalog, being static relationships between the largest limb segments or the change in one angle between two frames. Two structural repairs make that safe. The anchor frame is no longer 'hand furthest back', a depth criterion the arbitrary camera cannot measure, which risked all four pointers failing together and dropping a fifth of the evidence base in one block; it is now greatest hitting-elbow flexion, an in-plane quantity, with a per-pointer temporal fallback so the checkpoint degrades pointer by pointer. And the trunk pointer is inverted: the reviewer is right that requiring 15 degrees of vertical at maximum cocking taxed exactly the servers who load correctly, since elite backward lean there is 25 to 40 degrees. It survives as a gross-arch fault read with the sideways clause dropped, because camera azimuth mixes backward and sideways lean and no frame carries the azimuth to separate them. |
| `contact_point` - Contact | 29 | The contact instant and the two frames either side of it, at the highest available resolution. | Still the heaviest block, but the claim underneath it is rebuilt rather than restated. The net-tape comparison is deleted outright: the server stands 9 m behind the net, so hand and tape sit at wildly different depths and image height is not height — the projective error exceeded the quantity being measured, and the standing-height fallback inherited it because the athlete translates 1 to 2 m between the sampling frame and contact. In its place is a same-athlete, same-frame, same-depth ratio in which camera height, distance and lens all cancel. The apex read is widened from a single frame, which sat below the instrument's resolution near zero vertical velocity, to a band, and it now scores standing serves on a sign read instead of returning not_visible. arm_straight_at_contact is retired: nearly every server above beginner clears 160 degrees, so it inflated instead of discriminating, and it duplicated the same three landmarks in the same frame that the arm_swing delta already used. Its slot goes to the non-hitting arm at contact, a genuinely independent, purely vertical read. |
| `follow_through` - Finish and landing | 8 | Contact through the first ground contact after it. | Cut from 12 as the reviewer asked, because the ball has already left and three of the four old pointers were structurally broken: the serve-type gate was unreadable, the rotation read measured the wrong projection, and the travel-line read inverted on the assumed camera. All three are repaired or retired. Two of the reviewer's prescriptions are declined with reasons. First, a ball-outcome pointer: a served ball travels 2.5 to 4 m between context samples at 768px and is out of frame before it reaches the net in most clips, so scoring where it landed would recreate exactly the never-met, high-not_visible class this revision exists to remove; the weight cut is applied instead. Second, folding landing quality into an unscored safety note is not available under a fixed four-pointer shape, so it stays scored but is split into two single-condition reads, each with an explicit not_visible rule for the common case where the landing falls outside the analysed window. |

**Retired**

- `toss_stays_in_hitting_lane` - It required a behind-the-server or front-on camera while its sibling required a sideline camera, which are mutually exclusive positions, so at most one of the two could ever score and half an 18-weight checkpoint dropped out of every clip by construction. At the common 30 to 60 degree oblique both axes were foreshortened and neither was safe, and no frame carries the azimuth that would tell the model which test it was entitled to run. Collapsed into toss_clear_of_the_head, which judges ball against head and acromion on whichever axis carries the separation and therefore always scores.
- `lands_inside_endline` - Redundant with forward_drive_into_court: its own test called itself a coarser read of the same forward travel and its fallback was that pointer with footprints substituted for hips, which made forward travel worth half the checkpoint while the plant and the jump split the rest. Its primary reference also fails where the catalog most needs it: the painted endline must be identified under oblique handheld perspective, and on sand there is no painted line at all. Its share is now spent on the two halves of the plant.
- `travel_line_stays_straight` - Axis inversion. It compared footprints on the court's lateral axis, which from the sideline camera the rest of the catalog assumes runs into the image as depth, while the image's left-right axis is the athlete's forward drive. It did not degrade to not_visible; it returned a confidently inverted verdict, marking the athlete with the best forward drive as the most off-line. The fallback carried the identical defect. Off-line travel is only readable from a behind-the-server camera, which the frames give no way to detect.
- `arm_straight_at_contact` - Effectively every server above absolute beginner contacts at 160 degrees or more, because extension is what puts the hand on the ball at reach, so inside a 30-weight checkpoint it handed out a quarter of the heaviest block for free and masked the differences the score exists to expose. It also used the same three landmarks in the same frame as the arm_swing delta, charging one measurement twice. Contact-frame extension is now read once, as part of contact_reach_above_hips, and the swing's opening is read as a delta.
- `consistent_toss` - Repeatability cannot be judged from one rep. Same defect measured at 84 percent not_visible on attack/repeatable_height and 67 percent on set/repeatable_arc. The surviving half of the idea, whether this toss is higher than the swing needs, lives in toss_height_controlled as a single-rep measurement.
- `lifted_no_spin` - Ball spin is not resolvable at 768px on a small fast object at sideline distance. This is the attack/topspin_snap failure class at 84 percent not_visible.
- `wrist_snap` - Wrist action at contact needs sub-frame temporal resolution on the most motion-blurred object in the image. attack/topspin_snap and attack/fast_whip were never once marked met. The distal multiplication it reached for is now read honestly as a configuration change in elbow_opens_into_contact.
- `firm_open_hand` - Hand shape and rigidity need a close or frontal view the camera model does not guarantee, on the smallest and most blur-prone landmark in the frame. The terminal mechanics it proxied are read at arm-path scale in arm_finishes_low.
- `repeatable_contact` - A multi-rep claim, unjudgeable from one rep. The contact frame instead carries four independent single-rep reads: reach ratio, position relative to the shoulder, timing against the top of the rise, and the non-hitting arm.
- `stable_head` - Gaze is not resolvable with the athlete at a quarter of frame height and needs a facial view the arbitrary camera does not provide. The one legible head signal, backward tilt with a toss behind the head, is folded into the corroborating reads on toss_clear_of_the_head and contact_in_front.
- `square_finish` - Folded into hitting_shoulder_comes_through, which tests the same idea against a defined starting orientation instead of an undefined notion of facing the court, and now does so through shoulder-line tilt, which the projection preserves, rather than transverse rotation, which it does not.
- `body_alignment` - The checkpoint was retired earlier and stays retired: its content was posture claims with no frame anchor. Each surviving piece is scored where it is actually visible — trunk arch at the load frame in arm_swing, the loaded plant split across two pointers in approach_and_jump, and trunk and shoulder behaviour at the finish in follow_through.

## Passing / serve-receive (`pass`)

| Checkpoint | Weight | Phase | Why this weight |
|---|---:|---|---|
| `contact_control` - Contact | 24 | Contact: the 3-6 frames straddling ball contact, all inside the ~30fps dense window | Still holds the strongest published predictor in the skill: receiver displacement through the contact window raised reception-error odds with a large effect (Paulo et al. 2016, Exp(B)=10.60), and it is the best-sampled phase in the product because frames run at ~30fps here by construction, so frame-count language inside this window is unambiguous. Cut from 26 to 24 because the reviewer is right that arrival quality is partly re-read here (body_stopped is the contact-frame echo of the travel that footwork_to_ball scores), so the credit for arriving well is now split with footwork rather than banked twice at contact. |
| `platform_angle` - Platform build | 22 | Platform set through the first frames after contact: from the ball still clearly in flight to 2-3 frames past contact | A plane that is not assembled and rigid before the ball arrives has no defined face, so nothing downstream can be aimed. Coaching consensus rather than a controlled result, and labelled as such, but it is the mechanism behind the fault that most separates an inconsistent passer from a stable one. Cut from 24 to 22 to fund the footwork raise; every pointer now reads either a coarse silhouette feature or a ratio of the athlete's own segments, so none of it depends on where the phone stood. |
| `angle_to_target` - Direction control | 12 | Platform set through release: the last frames before contact and the 2-3 frames after | Cut from 18 to 12, which is the reviewer's call and correct: three of the four old pointers keyed on a target direction the frames never establish, and because not_visible is excluded from scoring, an 18-point checkpoint that goes blank silently reweights the rest of the rubric instead of failing loudly. The rebuilt pointers are all target-free, but the weight stays at 12 until the checkpoint clears an observed-rate floor of at least 80% non-not_visible over 30 labelled real reps, at which point restoring it to 16-18 is an evidence-backed change rather than an assertion. The 6 freed points went to footwork (4) and posture (2). |
| `body_posture` - Posture and base | 20 | Arrival through release: from the final plant to 2-3 frames after contact | Kapidzic and Huremovic tie centre-of-gravity height in the final phase to passing accuracy, and the base is the postural precondition that lets a platform angle survive the impulse of a fast serve. **Retired**

- `centered_midline` - Scores the wrong standard. The research names over-committing to midline position against a fast serve as its own fault: at high serve speed the athlete correctly trades centre-line arrival for arrival time and solves direction with the platform angle. What survives is moves_feet_not_reach, which now states its missed threshold explicitly as a base failure (a foot off the ground, or a lunge beyond two shoulder widths) rather than leaving missed to be inferred by default, which had quietly reintroduced the penalty this retirement removed.
- `pace_matched` - Requires incoming and outgoing ball speed and the serve type, none of which are measurable from stills without ball-velocity resolution. It is the passing analogue of attack/fast_whip, which returned not_visible 74% of the time. The observable half of the concept, that the athlete adds no uncalibrated energy, is carried by quiet_platform, now measured wrist-relative-to-shoulder so body rise cancels out.
- `hittable_arc` - Judges the ball after it leaves the platform, which needs ball tracking beyond the analyzed window and a visible setter target. That is outcome, not mechanics, and the catalog is deliberately mechanics-only. The mechanical causes it stood in for are covered by platform_redirects_off_serve_line and contact_height_in_band.
- `controlled_arc` - A restatement of hittable_arc in a second checkpoint, with the same dependency on tracking a departing ball to a target that is frequently off-screen. Retiring both removes one duplicated outcome measurement rather than two independent ones.
- `low_spin_off` - Needs ball-spin resolution from a handheld phone at sideline distance, which the constraints rule out outright and which put attack/topspin_snap in the never-marked-met class at 84% not_visible. Nothing about it is recoverable at this evidence quality.
- `faces_ball_angles_target` - Retired rather than rewritten. Its successor restated the platform-openness pointer with a different body segment and inherited the same target direction the frames never establish, so one checkpoint spent two of four slots charging a single unprovable fault twice. It also keyed on lead-foot direction, the least readable landmark in the shot: a shoe is about 35px at contact resolution, 18px on context frames, and its azimuth is unrecoverable under occlusion, sand, or a low camera. The freed slot went to head_still_through_contact, which is orthogonal, target-free, and reads off the largest landmark available.
- `shoulders_forward` - Folded into athletic_base_posture rather than kept as its own pointer. Its successor read the same shoulder/hip/foot geometry in the same frame as the knee pointer and pulled the opposite way, requiring the forward trunk hinge that the other demoted, so a correctly hinged passer was guaranteed to lose points on one of the two. It also asked for a vertical dropped from image gravity, which an arbitrarily rolled handheld camera does not supply; the merged pointer derives the vertical from the athlete's own ankle-to-hip axis.

**Retired**

- `quiet_contact` - Asks for sound. Frames carry no audio and never will under this pipeline. Measured at 89% not_visible, the worst in the catalog, and the 11% of verdicts that were not not_visible are the model inferring a sound from pictures, which is worse than abstaining.
- `repeatable_arc` - Structurally impossible from one rep. Measured at 67% not_visible. Repeatability can only ever be a cross-analysis feature computed over an athlete's stored history, not a pointer against a single frame sequence.
- `fingerpad_contact` - Needs the hands to occupy a meaningful fraction of the contact frame with an unobstructed view. At sideline distance with the athlete a quarter of frame height the hands are a small pixel cluster and are frequently behind the ball from the camera's side. Measured at 67% not_visible. The underlying signal now rides on wrists_level_at_release, which is a single-axis vertical comparison at a scale the camera can resolve.
- `no_carry` - Contact length is a temporal-resolution failure, not a camera-angle one, so no angle fix rescues it: at 30fps a legal and a marginally illegal contact differ by one or two frames and the window is not guaranteed to straddle contact cleanly. Measured at 67% not_visible. It is also surface-dependent in a way a single pointer cannot carry, since beach setters hold contact deliberately to strip spin and the fault there is unevenness, not duration.
- `still_head` - Eye tracking is unresolvable. At a quarter of frame height the eyes are a few pixels and gaze direction is not recoverable from a handheld phone at sideline distance. Head stability alone, stripped of the eye claim, adds nothing beyond stacked_posture.
- `right_foot_lead` - Naming a specific foot requires knowing which side of the net the athlete is on and which way they are facing, neither of which is reliable from an arbitrary camera angle, so the pointer punished correct left-side and beach mechanics. Its successor feet_split_at_the_plant now drops the stagger clause as well, because stagger and stance width are orthogonal ground-plane directions and one azimuth can show at most one of them.
- `gives_hitter_time` - Requires the hitter and their approach to be in frame and identifiable, which is not guaranteed at sideline distance and is frequently false when the target is outside the crop. It also duplicates what leaves_rising and stays_off_the_band already read off the ball itself, which is the reliably visible object.
- `shoulders_on_departure_line` - Punished a legal, common, high-level outcome outright: on a back set the ball leaves roughly 180 degrees from the chest, and on a deliberate off-shoulder front set the shoulders are not square by design, with no escape clause in the thresholds. The geometry was unrecoverable anyway, comparing a projected shoulder line with a projected ball path from an uncalibrated arbitrary azimuth where a true 20-degree offset can project anywhere from 5 to 70 degrees, over a post-release window sampled as low as 6fps. Its slot now holds shoulders_level_at_contact, an image-plane rotation that survives any azimuth, and the single surviving departure read is release/hands_follow_the_ball, reduced to a signed binary that a back set satisfies.
- `arrives_early` - The same construct as drift_stopped measured twice inside one checkpoint: an athlete with three consecutive settled frames before contact cannot then travel half a hip width across it. Together they handed 12 of 100 points to one fact. It also had no numeric threshold at all where its sibling did, so the two disagreed by construction on borderline reps, and it scored correct jump-set footwork as missed because a jump setter approaches through the ball and is never settled with hips no longer translating before takeoff.
- `commits_after_the_read` - The observable_test and the thresholds measured different quantities: the test asked for the change in ball-to-midline offset between commitment and contact, while both thresholds referenced only the commitment-frame offset and never used the contact offset, so evaluators following the test and evaluators following the thresholds return different verdicts on the same rep. Its discriminator, closing the gap 'with a lean rather than a full lateral reach', was undefined. And the quantity itself, lateral ball-to-midline offset, is already scored by ball_over_the_midline at the frame that matters.
- `contact_at_peak_of_lift` - The met/partial boundary sat at a parabolic apex, where vertical velocity is zero by definition and one 33ms frame either side differs by about 5mm of hip travel. At a 220px stature in a 1568px frame that is under 0.05px, two orders of magnitude below resolution and far below uncompensated handheld jitter. Every verdict was a coin flip presented as a measurement. The jump-versus-standing distinction it gestured at is now handled where it is actually observable, inside release/legs_drive_through_contact, as a binary feet-off-the-ground fork rather than as a scored pointer that would abstain on every correct standing set.
- `absorbs_the_ball` - The failure no_carry was retired for, re-admitted under a new name. Contact lasts 50 to 150ms, and the pointer asked whether elbow flexion held or closed inside that interval: a few degrees over a forearm imaged at roughly 45px, i.e. one to three pixels of landmark motion under blur. It is a limb-velocity read, not a shape read. Its met threshold was also free, satisfied by nearly any set including a rigid punch set, and the cue is contested, since elite setters minimise dwell and 'sinks in for a beat' rewards the slower amateur pattern. The hands-firmness signal now rides on two static single-frame shape reads instead, elbows_flexed_at_contact and release/arms_open_through_the_push.
- `balanced_finish` - Scored after the ball had already left the hands, so nothing it measured changed the set that was produced, and a recovery step is frequently the correct response to a hard-run-down ball, which made it a tax on the difficulty of the pass. It was also the fourth reading of the base-and-balance construct in one catalog. Its claimed high visibility was the reverse of the truth: those frames sit in the 6fps 768px tail where the athlete is roughly 108px tall and the camera is panning after the ball, and 'no fall in any direction' again included the invisible depth axis.

**Retired**

- `power_followthrough` - Retired as a checkpoint. Follow-through is a consequence of the contact that precedes it, not an independent determinant, and every stills-readable version of it collapses into either arm speed (unmeasurable at 30fps and at a quarter of frame height) or a muscle-tone judgement no still can carry. Its slot goes to landing_recovery, which carries the real consequence in this skill: spike landings are the leading ACL mechanism in volleyball. The legitimate power content it was reaching for is rebuilt upstream as arm_swing/trunk_rotation_forward, arm_swing/elbow_leads_hand and contact_quality/arm_extended_at_contact, all of which are silhouette-scale and attainable.
- `attack_swing_style_labels` - Any pointer that classifies the swing into a named style (bow-and-arrow, circular, snap) is retired outright and none is rebuilt. Style classification needs continuous motion that a 30fps burst cannot supply, and a 2026 survey of 175 competitive players found no injury-rate difference between bow-and-arrow (32.8%) and circular (28.9%) styles (p=0.63), while the style mix flips by surface (about 39.5% circular indoors versus about 13.3% on the beach). The joint position matters and the style label does not, so the observable content lives on in arm_swing/arm_load_height.
- `long_low_penultimate` - Retired. Stride length is measured along the approach axis, which for an outside hitter runs diagonally at the net and is therefore mostly a depth distance from both the sideline and the backcourt camera. The 1.5x test compared two differently foreshortened projections taken at different depths, so perspective alone could flip met to missed, and the hip-width ruler did not fix it because it is a frontal-plane measure applied to a sagittal distance. The braking function the long penultimate step serves is now read directly and in-frame as approach_plant/braking_posture, which needs no length measurement at all.
- `takeoff_behind_ball` - Retired as redundant. It used the identical construction to contact_quality/ball_in_front_of_shoulder (vertical through the hitting acromion, ball centre, half-ball-width threshold, net side counts as front), separated by about 0.5s of flight in which the relationship barely changes, so one fault was charged twice across two checkpoints. It was also the weaker instance: it read the ball in a 768px context frame instead of the 1568px burst, and a high set at takeoff is frequently cropped out of a handheld frame that is following the hitter. The horizontal-position construct is now carried once, in the burst.
- `hand_above_ball_middle` - Retired rather than rebuilt, on the same grounds that sank topspin_snap at 84% not_visible. Physical contact lasts roughly 10ms against a 33ms sampling grid, so the sampled frame usually shows an approaching or departing hand rather than hand-on-ball, and the stated carve-outs (hand occluding the ball, motion blur, camera behind the hitter) describe the modal clip rather than the exception. What makes a ball hittable down is already carried by contact_above_head, ball_in_front_of_shoulder and arm_extended_at_contact; the freed slot went to a lateral-axis contact-position read that is scoreable from exactly the camera geometries where ball_in_front_of_shoulder must abstain.
- `foot_contact_ordering_tests` - The whole class of sub-frame contact-ordering reads is retired, both at takeoff and at landing. A real foot stagger is 20-50ms at takeoff and 30-80ms at landing while the context grid is 167ms, so 'no frame shows one foot down while the other is airborne' was satisfied by sampling sparsity in nearly every clip regardless of technique, making it a free point that measured the sampler. The takeoff instance additionally scored correct technique as a fault on slide and one-foot quick attacks. The two slots survive as static posture reads that the frame rate can actually support: approach_plant/plant_feet_together and landing_recovery/landing_load_shared, both of which abstain on approaches and surfaces where a single-leg action is correct.

| Checkpoint | Weight | Phase | Why this weight |
|---|---:|---|---|
| `read_timing` - Read & timing | 20 | Ready through takeoff, anchored on two ball events: the apex of the set and the attacker's contact (roughly the first 70% of the window) | CAMERA-GEOMETRY PROTOCOL (applies to the whole catalog; classify the clip ONCE, before judging any pointer). Class A, net-edge-on: the camera sits within roughly 30 degrees of the net plane, the net band foreshortens toward a point or a short steep line, and the two courts sit to the left and right of frame. Crossing the net is lateral image motion; travel ALONG the net is depth. Travel along the net is lateral image motion; crossing the net is depth. Class C, oblique: both axes carry some lateral component and neither cleanly, so judge only unambiguous cases. One lens recovers neither depth axis, so every pointer below names the classes it is valid in and abstains in the others instead of answering confidently on an axis the view collapsed. The catalog is now built so the two heaviest checkpoints do NOT demand opposite phone positions: penetration is class-A weighted, footwork and body position are class-B weighted, and read_timing and landing are mostly class-independent, so every checkpoint keeps at least two judgeable pointers from any legal camera position. Sampling is about 30fps only through the contact window and can fall to 6fps elsewhere, so when the frame spacing around an event is wider than the band being tested, return not_visible rather than converting frame counts at an assumed 30fps. | `hand_penetration` - Hand penetration | 22 | Press and penetration at the net, read at the peak frame and across the 0.1s either side of the attacker's contact | Depth over the plane is still what converts a block into removed court, but the old 26 rested on a claim that inverted the geometry: the net tape is a long clean line exactly in the view (net-face-on) where crossing it is unrecoverable depth, and it foreshortens exactly in the view (net-edge-on) where crossing it is a clean lateral measurement. | `landing_recovery` - Landing & recovery | 13 | Landing and the first movement after it, from touchdown to about 0.5s later (final ~10-20% of the window) | Stated plainly, because the previous version summed two objectives into one number without saying so: this checkpoint is scored against injury exposure, not against whether the rep stopped the ball. Landing is the sport's dominant acute injury mechanism and most block landings that hurt someone hurt them at the net, so it keeps a real slice, but the slice is cut to 13 so a player reading the score knows the large majority of it answers 'did this block work' and one named, smaller part answers 'is this landing going to cost you an ankle'. The content is rebuilt to fit the frames it actually gets: the forefoot-versus-heel clause is gone (5-10px discrimination in the blurriest frames of the window, and no heel strike at all on sand), the knee-angle delta is gone in favour of a vertical hip drop, the centerline pointer is gone (near-universally met indoors, and on sand it deducted for legal play, since passing under the net is not a fault there unless it interferes), and the reload pointer now abstains when the rally ended on the block, because standing still after a stuff is correct behaviour and the old version scored the skill's best outcome as missed. |

**Retired**

- `fingers_spread_firm` - Finger spread and firmness are hand-level detail on a hand that may occupy a few dozen pixels, from an angle with no guaranteed view of the palm side, frequently occluded by the net band itself. Same evidence class as set/fingerpad_contact and attack/topspin_snap, which run 67 to 84 percent not_visible. The usable part of the signal survives as gross arm geometry in hands_ahead_of_shoulders, and the outcome it was proxying (does a touch go down) is now measured directly against the ball in hands_above_ball_at_contact.
- `sealed_gap` - Hand separation and seam integrity are angle-dependent in the opposite direction to everything else here: a net-edge-on view, which is the one view where the penetration pointers work at all, collapses both hands onto nearly the same image column and cannot resolve the gap. It is also undefined on beach, which is one on one and has no seam. Guaranteed high not_visible, so it would silently shrink the evidence base.
- `tight_core` - No named landmark and no threshold two coaches would agree on. What it was reaching for, in-flight control, is measured directly by no_drift_along_net, square_on_arrival, and arms_extended_at_peak, each of which names specific landmarks and a fixed or self-referential reference.
- `eyes_open_neutral` - Gaze and head orientation cannot be honestly measured here. On a subject a quarter of frame height, at an arbitrary handheld angle, head yaw cannot be separated from trunk rotation and cannot be resolved to a fixation target. The underlying competence shows up as its gross-body consequence in reads_before_apex, which is anchored on a ball event rather than on a gaze.
- `off_the_net` - Redundant with takeoff_distance, which measures the same net gap at the one frame where it decides the outcome. Judging the gap continuously through transit adds no discriminating information and adds angle noise, and the gap is only measurable at all on net-edge-on views, which takeoff_distance now states explicitly.
- `no_net_touch` - A net touch is an outcome of drift and takeoff distance, both of which are now scored at their causes, and it is near-universally met, so it spends a pointer slot without separating anyone. The landing slot it would have taken now carries knees_track_over_feet, which measures the actual injury mechanism and does separate athletes.

The old counterfactual clause about a genuinely out-of-reach ball is removed. | Hips over or inside the ankle span through the post-contact frames, or a continuous front-side slide with the trunk long. | One recovery step, or a backward lean the athlete arrests before the trunk passes the base. | high | balanced_through_contact |
| `reloads_base` | Get back down into a stance instead of standing up and watching | In the final sampled frames of the analyzed window, measure hip height as a fraction of the athlete's own standing height H, self-contained. No comparison is made to pre-attack context frames, which the window is not guaranteed to contain. The abstention is explicit and mandatory rather than left to judgement: not_visible whenever the window ends less than 500ms after the defender's contact or fewer than three post-contact frames are sampled, because re-forming a base takes roughly that long and scoring a truncated clip taxes the athlete for the sampler's boundary. Also not_visible when the athlete is occluded in the final frames. | Hip height at or below 0.52H with both feet on the ground in the final sampled frames. | Hip height 0.52H-0.60H, or a base formed only in the very last sampled frame. | low | reloads_athletic_base |

**Retired**

- `wide_base` - Collapsed into athletic_base. Its own measurement was broken as written: ankle separation over shoulder width is a ratio between two segments in different planes, so a staggered stance viewed from the side projects ankles at full length and shoulders to near zero (a narrow base scores met) while the same stance viewed face-on inverts it. The surviving idea, stance width against the athlete's own standing height with an explicit abstention when the ankles overlap in image space, is now the second clause of athletic_base.
- `chest_over_knees` - Dropped entirely. It needed a world-vertical reference that a handheld phone with arbitrary roll does not supply (20 degrees is inside typical handheld tilt) and an out-of-plane correction, so a good hinge shot face-on scored missed. Its intra-body rebuild would still have measured what hip sink already measures: at the base depths real defenders show, forward trunk lean and hip sink co-vary, and keeping both counted one behaviour twice inside one checkpoint.
- `trunk_faces_attacker` - Retired. Shoulder-line yaw to a 30-degree tolerance means resolving depth-wise foreshortening of a ~50px segment on a jersey-covered torso with the arms crossing it, which is the same quantity that got eyes_stay_on_ball retired, one notch coarser. It also required the attacker and the ringed defender in frame together, which sideline phone framing often does not deliver. Read quality is now carried by positioned_in_the_ball_line, no_early_bail, grounded_at_swing and reaction_start.
- `up_and_on_feet` - Retired. Its own thresholds partitioned the space into auto-met and abstain: a dig played on the feet met it by construction, which is most reps, and on emergency digs, the only reps where getting up fast carries information, the window ends before the athlete could plausibly rise and it returned not_visible. A pointer that can only add, and adds identically to everyone, is the mirror of the invisible-tax failure. Its weight moved to the platform and delivery checkpoint.
- `faces_the_play` - Retired. Same unrecoverable shoulder-line yaw as trunk_faces_attacker, measured in the lower-resolution post-contact frames, and its met condition (rotating toward the ball's path OR toward the net) covered most of the compass from a backcourt defender's position, so it was close to unfalsifiable wherever it was readable at all. Recovery is now carried by balanced_through_contact and reloads_base.
- `weight_forward` - Previously retired and still out. It asks for heel height, which is unreadable at a quarter of frame height and invisible on sand, and the underlying coaching point (toe-loaded stance versus flat-foot hip hinge) is genuinely contested with no volleyball-specific evidence adjudicating it.
- `in_the_lane` - Previously retired and still out. It needs the blockers, the court lines and the team's defensive system in the same frames as the ringed defender, and it cannot be judged at all in the two-player beach game. Its recoverable part now lives in no_early_bail (discipline) and positioned_in_the_ball_line (where the defender actually stood).
- `full_effort_extension` - Previously retired and still out. It could only be met when the ball was out of two-arm reach, so on ordinary reps it was either not_visible or a forced miss, which is the invisible-tax pattern exactly. Reach versus arrive is now scored positively by reach_inside_shoulders and technique choice by technique_matches_reach.
- `stays_low_moving` - Previously retired and still out. It demanded posture judgement across the whole travel window, the part of the rep most often occluded by other players' legs at sideline distance. Its signal is now split between athletic_base before the swing and stays_low_through_contact, which measures the same posture at two well-defined instants instead of continuously.
- `eyes_stay_on_ball` - Previously retired and still out. Gaze direction is not recoverable at this resolution: at 768px a head carries a few pixels of yaw information and pupils are not resolvable at any framing the product guarantees. No orientation pointer inherits it in this revision; the two that tried have both been retired.

# Vollyio 120-Pointer Catalog v2: Editor's Assembly

**Status:** specification for implementation. Every pointer key below is exact and is the string the model returns and `deriveMetric` looks up.

**Scope defect, read first:** this assembly covers **five** skills. The brief names six. **No rebuilt `attack` catalog was supplied.** Attack is still on the v1 catalog and still carries `topspin_snap`, `repeatable_height` and `fast_whip`, three of the five never-met pointers, on the lowest-scoring skill in the product (median ~63). Section 5 states why v2 cannot ship for five skills while attack stays on v1.

---

## 1. What changed and why

The measured failure is not that the rubric is wrong. It is that a third of it is not answerable from the evidence the product actually collects, and the scoring rule turns that into a silent distortion rather than a loud one.

Three compounding faults:

**32% not_visible is not neutral.** `deriveMetric` excludes not_visible from the denominator, so an unanswerable pointer does not fail, it shrinks the evidence base. A checkpoint whose four pointers reduce to one visible pointer is scored on that one pointer at full weight. The score looks like a 100-point read and is a 25-point read. Nothing in the output distinguishes the two.

**The never-met class is a fixed tax.** `topspin_snap`, `repeatable_height`, `fast_whip`, `bow_and_arrow` and `relaxed_finish` were never once marked met across 600 verdicts. A pointer that can return partial or missed but never met cannot raise a score and can only lower it. Because they cluster on attack, the tax is not even uniform across skills: it is heaviest where scores are already lowest.

**Six failure classes account for nearly all of it, and all six are properties of the evidence, not of the wording.** Audio (frames carry none). Repeatability (one rep cannot demonstrate it). Ball spin and limb velocity (not resolvable on a small fast object at sideline distance). Gaze (a few pixels at a quarter of frame height). Hand-scale detail (8 to 12 px). Unknowable court context (target, rotation, system, an unmarked setter or attacker). No rewrite rescues a pointer in these classes, so 48 pointers are retired outright rather than reworded.

What replaces them, as a construction rule rather than a claim: **every surviving pointer reads either a vertical relation, a ratio of the athlete's own segments measured inside one frame, a coarse silhouette boundary, or a time interval taken from frame timestamps.** Those are the four things one uncalibrated handheld view actually gives. Projected joint angles survive only behind a foreshortening gate. Depth-axis quantities are either abstained on by camera class (block, set, pass) or replaced by an in-plane proxy.

Two further structural repairs that matter more than any individual pointer:

- **Circular family classification is removed.** Serve's `forward_drive_into_court` used to reclassify any athlete who travelled little into the standing family, so its own threshold could never fail. Family is now decided by an independent test (both feet off the ground at contact).
- **Abstention becomes loud.** Dig introduces the rule that a checkpoint with two or more not_visible pointers is reported as not assessable and its weight removed from the composite, with the player told which checkpoints were dropped. Section 3 promotes this catalog-wide. It is the only change here that fixes the *scoring* fault rather than the *observability* fault.

The honest claim about the result: the six impossible classes are gone, so the named worst offenders (89%, 84%, 84%, 74%, 67%, 67%) cannot recur. The overall not_visible rate cannot be forecast without re-labelling. Section 4 names where the remaining risk sits.

## 3. Cross-skill consistency

Six catalogs were designed independently and drifted. The drift is not cosmetic: the same construct is measured against different denominators, with different thresholds, and with two skills asserting that an instrument is usable while two others explicitly ban it. Below is the canonical registry, then the resolutions. **These resolutions are editor decisions and are part of the spec.**

### 3.1 Canonical unit registry

Every threshold in the catalog must be expressed in one of these, named exactly:

| Symbol | Definition | Replaces |
|---|---|---|
| **H** | Standing height, head crown to heel, from the frames where the athlete stands tallest | block "standing height", dig "H" |
| **T** | Trunk length, hip midpoint to shoulder midpoint | serve "torso length", pass "trunk length" |
| **S** | Shoulder width, imaged acromion to acromion | pass/dig "shoulder width" |
| **W** | Hip width | pass/set/block "hip width" |
| **D** | Ball diameter (**contact-tier frames only**; D is 15 to 25px on context frames) | serve/block "ball diameter" |
| **HD / HH** | Head width / head height (used only where the head is the largest reliable landmark) | set "head width", "head height" |
| **FL** | Foot length | serve "foot length" |
| **HS** | Hand span (block only, where nothing coarser resolves) | block "hand-span" |

Approximate reconciliation ratios, used **only** to check thresholds against each other and never to substitute one measurement for another: T ~ 0.30H, S ~ 0.23H, W ~ 0.14H, FL ~ 0.15H.

### 3.2 Camera classification, promoted catalog-wide

Block's A/B/C/D protocol is the only formal one. Set, pass, dig and serve each improvised a gate ("at or near its widest", "under about half its widest value", "shoulder line under 0.15H", "shoulder line under about half the width expected for this athlete's head size"). Three of those four are non-comparable and one is an absolute that varies with camera distance.

**Resolution.** One gate metric everywhere: **r_s = imaged S divided by that athlete's maximum imaged S across the sequence.** Face-on to the axis of interest: r_s >= 0.85. Edge-on: r_s <= 0.45. Oblique: 0.45 to 0.85. Block's net-relative A/B/C/D classes are the specialization of this to the net axis, and block already uses exactly 0.85 and 0.45 in `square_on_arrival`. Restate the gates in `pass/contact_in_front`, `pass/moves_feet_not_reach`, `set/ball_over_the_midline`, `set/stacked_posture`, `dig/positioned_in_the_ball_line` and `dig/reach_inside_shoulders` in terms of r_s. Dig's absolute "S < 0.15H" is dropped: it conflates foreshortening with camera distance.

Declared visibility, counted from the catalogs:

| Skill | high | medium | low | Largest structural risk |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| serve | 10 | 10 | 0 | Both `approach_and_jump` plant pointers gated on a ground-contact frame being sampled during a 100 to 200ms plant that can straddle the 30fps window. They co-fail, dropping 24 weight |
| pass | 7 | 13 | 0 | `angle_to_target` (12): `platform_redirects_off_serve_line` needs the ball's path resolvable pre-contact, and `shoulder_tilt_matches_ball_side` abstains outright on a midline ball. Both fail on a serve hit straight at the passer, dropping the checkpoint |
| set | 7 | 13 | 0 | The whole `footwork` block (18) is foot-dependent: `feet_split_at_the_plant` and `loaded_at_the_plant` both need ankles resolvable and co-fail on sand, on court markings, or behind traffic |
| block | 3 | 17 | 0 | See below. The camera-class partition is the single worst coverage finding in the catalog |
| dig | 10 | 9 | 1 | `read_and_reaction` (16): `reaction_start` and `first_step_direction` both abstain when the ball is within 1.5S of the midline, which is common. The rationale admits this |

**Block's camera-class problem is structural, not incidental.** Work it through under the 2-abstention rule:

- On a **class A** (net-edge-on) clip, five pointers abstain: `commits_without_reversal`, `braked_plant`, `no_drift_along_net`, `block_on_hitting_line`, `knees_track_over_feet`. `body_position` loses two of four and **the whole 22-weight checkpoint drops**.
- On a **class B** (net-face-on) clip, `hands_over_net` and `hands_ahead_of_shoulders` both abstain and **the whole 22-weight `hand_penetration` checkpoint drops**.
- On **class D** (net not locatable) roughly half the catalog abstains.

So on either legal camera position, block loses a 22-point checkpoint by construction. The rationale claims "every checkpoint keeps at least two judgeable pointers from any legal camera position", and that claim is false for `body_position` on class A. This must be resolved before block ships (Open Question 3).

**Other named risks:**

- `set/stays_off_the_band` needs the net band in crop; its own rationale says this is "common[ly]" false while the camera follows an athlete several metres off the net.
- `set/stacked_posture` has a hard view gate: near front-on or near behind returns not_visible.
- `dig/reloads_base` is the only pointer in the catalog declared **low** visibility, and it abstains whenever the window ends less than 500ms after contact. Given a 2 to 3s window centred on the defender's contact, that will be frequent.
- `serve/follow_through` is four medium pointers, all four with window-truncation abstention rules. Cheap at 8 weight, but the checkpoint will drop often.
- Four pass pointers and most of block and set depend on per-frame timestamps existing in the frame payload. If they do not, those pointers degrade to the ball-distance fallback or to not_visible (Open Question 4).

**Never-met audit.** No surviving pointer is structurally unable to return met. The three closest to the line, and worth watching in the first label pass: `block/hands_over_net` (needs class A *and* both wrists past the column *and* shoulders behind), `dig/reloads_base`, `set/stays_off_the_band`.

**The inverse risk, which the rebuild has not eliminated.** Several new pointers look near-auto-met and will add a constant rather than discriminate, which is the `arm_straight_at_contact` failure in new clothing: `serve/toss_hand_finishes_high`, `pass/head_still_through_contact`, `block/hands_up_before_takeoff`, `dig/grounded_at_swing`, `set/finishes_tall`. Measure the met rate on the first 30 labelled reps per skill; anything above ~90% met is a constant and should be re-thresholded or replaced.

**What can and cannot be claimed.** The six impossible classes are gone, so the named worst offenders cannot recur. The overall not_visible rate **cannot be forecast** without re-labelling, and the abstention rule will *raise* the not-observed rate at checkpoint level while lowering it at pointer level. That is the intended trade: loud abstention over silent reweighting.

### 5.2 Why stored analyses cannot be recomputed

Three independent reasons, any one of which is sufficient:

1. **Verdicts are stored against old pointer keys, and the lookup is by exact key.** `deriveMetric` builds `byKey` from the reported pointers and then maps over the *catalog*, so a reported key not in the new catalog is silently dropped and a catalog key not in the reported set parses to `not_visible` (`parsePointerStatus` returns `not_visible` for anything unrecognized, including `undefined`). Replaying a v1 result against the v2 catalog therefore yields four not_visible pointers per checkpoint, `observed: false` everywhere, and `overall: null`. It does not produce a wrong number; it produces no number.

2. **There is no remap, even where the key name survives.** `serve/contact_in_front` exists in both versions. The v1 test was "contact in front of the hitting shoulder" with no threshold; the v2 test is a full ball diameter ahead of the acromion line with a stated direction convention and a near-end-on fallback. A stored `met` answers a different question. Sixteen keys are in this position across the five skills. Remapping them would launder v1 judgements into v2 numbers, which is worse than having no number.

3. **`analyses` rows are immutable by design.** `docs/security.md` records no update grant on `analyses`: "a row is immutable after creation". A backfill would require the service-role client, and rule 10 forbids adding a third importer without a documented reason that the operation cannot run as the signed-in player. "We want to rewrite history" is not that reason.

**Therefore:** stored analyses keep their v1 `result` and their v1 `scale_version` stamp (`app/api/analyze/route.ts:368`) and are read-only historical records. The breakdown UI must render a v1 result with its v1 checkpoint labels, so the label lookup has to be version-aware or the old labels must be retained as a frozen map. A v1 breakdown must not be rendered against v2 checkpoint names.

### 5.3 The observability rule change

Adopting dig's 2-abstention rule catalog-wide is a change to `deriveMetric`:

```
current: observed = visible.length >= 1
new:     observed = visible.length >= 3   (of 4)
```

Downstream consequences, all of which must be measured before adoption:

- `weightedOverall` drops the checkpoint's weight from the denominator, so `coveragePct` falls and more reps trip `lowConfidence` (currently `coveragePct < 60`).
- `updateRating` weights the EWMA step by `coveragePct/100`, so low-coverage reps move ratings less. That is the correct behaviour, but the effective learning rate drops across the board.
- `overall: null` becomes more common (three or more checkpoints unassessable), and the route then falls back to the model's whole-clip read, which is *not* pointer-derived. That fallback path is now load-bearing in a way it was not before and needs review.
- The player-visible requirement ("shown which checkpoints were dropped") is new UI, not just a scoring change.

Given block's camera-class problem (Section 4), this rule and block's weighting cannot be decided independently.

### 5.4 Existing `skill_ratings`

Migration 053 already built the mechanism for exactly this, so **no data migration is needed and no backfill should be written.**

- `skill_ratings.scale_version` is nullable with no default; NULL means version 1 by construction.
- `nextRating` returns the raw score (re-seeds) when `prev.scale_version ?? 1` differs from `SCALE_VERSION`.
- On the first v2 analysis per `(user_id, skill, discipline)`, the rating discards its history and seeds at the new score. Rows for skills the player has not re-repped keep their v1 value and their v1 stamp.

Three gaps this leaves:

1. **`overallScore` (`lib/ratings.ts:49`) has no scale awareness.** It averages ratings across skills. After the bump it will average v1 and v2 ratings until every skill is re-repped. Either exclude stale-version ratings from the overall, or label the overall as provisional until all six skills carry the current version. This needs deciding before ship.
2. **`analyses_count` is not reset when the rating re-seeds.** A re-seeded rating with a count of 40 reads as well-established evidence that it no longer is. Decide whether the count re-seeds too.
3. **The player sees an unexplained jump.** A rating re-seeding from an EWMA of 40 reps to a single rep's score is a large, unexplained move. This needs copy, not just code.

### 5.5 What must be re-labelled and re-evaluated before adoption

Everything. The labelled set is keyed to v1 pointer keys and carries no v2 verdicts.

1. **Re-label the pro regression set.** `evals/cases-pro-regression/` holds 12 videos across ~17 part files, all with v1 pointer verdicts. Every case needs fresh per-pointer labels against the v2 catalog. `evals/LABELING.md` and `scripts/label-evals.mjs` need rewriting first, because the labelling instructions embed the v1 tests.
2. **Meet the 30-rep floor per skill.** `pass/angle_to_target` is explicitly held at weight 12 pending "at least 80% non-not_visible over 30 labelled real reps". That gate cannot be evaluated without 30 labelled reps for pass, and the same floor should apply to every checkpoint flagged in Section 4.
3. **Measure the not_visible rate per pointer and per checkpoint**, and specifically the co-abstention rate for the named pairs (serve plant pair, pass angle pair, set footwork pair, dig reaction pair, block by camera class). `scripts/eval-coverage.mjs` needs updating for the new keys and for the checkpoint-level abstention rule.
4. **Run the never-met and always-met audit** on the same set: any pointer at 0% met or above ~90% met over 30 reps is a defect, not a result.
5. **Classify camera class per eval clip** and report block's coverage stratified by class. Do not report a single block coverage number.
6. **Re-run the A/B harness** (`evals/ab-2026-08-04/`) with v2 as an arm, and compare the score distribution per skill against the v1 baseline. The median shift is expected and is not the failure signal; the failure signals are checkpoint-drop rate and any skill whose distribution collapses toward the 30 floor or the 100 ceiling.
7. **Do not ship until attack is rebuilt** (Section 6, Question 1).

Deployment order: catalog plus weights plus `SCALE_VERSION` plus `deriveMetric` in one commit; UI label version-awareness in the same deploy; no database migration required.

## 6. Open questions for the owner

1. **Attack is missing, and it is the blocking one.** No rebuilt attack catalog was supplied. Attack carries three of the five never-met pointers and is the lowest-scoring skill. Shipping v2 for five skills leaves attack on v1 keys and v1 weights, which means `overallScore` averages a v1 attack rating with five v2 ratings indefinitely, and the never-met tax survives on exactly the skill where it is heaviest. Is attack in flight, and does v2 ship as a six-skill unit or not at all?

2. **Adopt the 2-abstention checkpoint-drop rule catalog-wide, or leave it dig-only?** It is the fix for the actual scoring fault, and it is also the change most likely to produce a lot of null overalls and low-confidence flags. It cannot be decided from the desk; it needs the coverage numbers from 5.5 step 3 first. If it is adopted, what is the acceptable rate of "checkpoint not assessable" before the product feels broken?

3. **Block's camera-class partition guarantees a 22-weight checkpoint drops on every legal camera position.** Three options: accept it and let block routinely score on 78 of 100 weight; reweight so no single class can lose a whole checkpoint (which means moving pointers between checkpoints, not just moving numbers); or ask the player for camera position at upload and refuse or warn on class D. Option three is the only one that removes the problem rather than absorbing it, and it is a product change.

4. **Are per-frame timestamps available in the frame payload?** Block and set mandate them, four pass pointers depend on them, and dig annotates in them. If they are not currently emitted, that is a pipeline change and a precondition for this catalog, not a nice-to-have. If they are only approximate, the 0.07s two-foot thresholds are not measurable.

5. **Weight is now the primary lever and none of it is fitted.** serve `contact_point` at 29 and set `body_alignment` at 32 are the two heaviest blocks in the catalog and both are asserted from reasoning, not fitted to labels. `docs/decisions.md` D-034 says weights are a code-side calibration knob tuned against labelled cases. Do these get fitted before ship, or do they ship asserted with fitting deferred?

6. **The `angle_to_target` conditional weight.** It is held at 12 pending an 80% non-not_visible floor over 30 labelled reps, with restoration to 16 to 18 promised if it clears. Who runs that check, on whose labels, and what happens if it fails: does the checkpoint get retired rather than re-weighted?

7. **Indoor and beach on one scale.** `skill_ratings` is keyed by `(user_id, skill, discipline)` and `METRICS` is deliberately shared across disciplines. Several v2 pointers branch on surface (serve `vertical_drive_off_the_ground` explicitly lowers the sand threshold; block `braked_plant` and `landing_recovery` mention sand) while dig `technique_matches_reach` explicitly refuses to branch, on the grounds that branching would give one discipline a constant bonus. Both positions are defensible and they cannot both be the standard. Which is it?

8. **Should the family fork be a stored attribute?** Serve forks jump/standing, set forks jump/standing, block forks by camera class, dig forks platform/overhand. Today each fork lives inside a pointer's prose. Storing the fork on the analysis would let evals stratify (is the low score a standing-serve artefact?) and would let the UI explain the branch. It is also a schema addition on an immutable table.

9. **Serve's `follow_through` has two post-outcome pointers**, which violates the one-per-checkpoint cap proposed in Section 3.3. The checkpoint is only 8 weight, so the harm is small, and cutting one would leave three pointers in a four-pointer shape that every other checkpoint honours. Cut one and accept a three-pointer checkpoint, keep both and carve out an exception, or replace one with a pre-contact read?

10. **Are the anthropometric conversion ratios (T ~ 0.30H, S ~ 0.23H, W ~ 0.14H) acceptable for reconciling thresholds across skills?** They are population averages and they are wrong for individual athletes, particularly juniors. They are used here only to check that a 0.5W threshold in pass and a 0.1H threshold in dig are the same number. If that is not acceptable, every shared construct must be restated in one natively measured unit, which means re-deriving several thresholds from scratch.

11. **Does the scale itself need re-anchoring, and is this the moment?** `RAW_FLOOR = 30`, `RAW_CEILING = 100`, bands at 55/80/92. The v2 catalog is materially harder: the free pointers (`arm_straight_at_contact`, `no_net_touch`, `up_and_on_feet`) are gone, so a given athlete will score lower on identical footage. Migration 053's comment says the scale was already due for an intermediate-to-pro re-anchor. If the floor or the band boundaries move, doing it inside `SCALE_VERSION = 2` is free; doing it later costs a third version and a second rating re-seed for every player.

## Key collisions, complete set

One pointer key is reused across skills: **`contact_in_front`**, in `serve.contact_point`
and `pass.contact_control`. This is deliberate reuse of one construct rather than a
collision, and the two thresholds are consistent in meaning while differing in
landmark, as they must:

- `serve`: ball centre at least one ball diameter in front of the acromion line.
- `pass`: ball centre or wrists clearly ahead of the toe line, wrists clear of the
  trunk silhouette and above knee height.

Two checkpoint keys recur: `arm_swing` (serve 25, attack 18) and `landing_recovery`
(attack 16, block 13). Both are correctly skill-scoped: the phases differ (a serve's
load frame versus an attack's ascent-through-contact; an attack's two-foot landing
versus a blocker's landing and reload), and the weights differ for stated reasons.
No renaming is required.

**Block is the coverage risk to watch.** It carries only 3 high-visibility pointers

the designers' expectations and have never been tested against footage. Treat the
first labelled run as the test of this table, not as a formality.

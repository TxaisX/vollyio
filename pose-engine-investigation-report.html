What actually broke

The old engine used a two step process: first find every person in the frame, then work out the body position of each one separately. The replacement engine, MediaPipe, is built around photographing one prominent subject. When the swap happened, the second half was kept and the first half was dropped, on the assumption that the new engine did both.

It does not. Handed a whole frame of a busy court, it returns roughly one body, and not reliably the one you tapped.

How to read that

You give up 41 correct readings to remove 70 wrong ones. A wrong reading is not a blank space, it is the app confidently describing someone else's technique as yours. For a product whose whole promise is not making things up, that is probably the right trade, but it is a trade and it is your call rather than mine.

Defects found

Was live

Two measurements silently disabled

Both relied on body points the engine never produced. Averaging over missing points dragged their confidence below the cutoff, so they were dropped from every analysis. Nothing errored, so nobody knew.

Was live

The evaluation suite was passing itself

Saved test cases carried an expected score range of 0 to 100. Every possible score falls in that range, so the check ran, passed, and reported success forever, while the check that mattered was skipped in silence.

Would have shipped

The billing switch was a trapdoor

Turning billing on would have capped every user at one analysis for life, behind an upgrade button pointing nowhere, because no payment system exists. It now takes two switches and the wrong one alone does nothing.

Would have shipped

The honesty check would have stopped working

Tuned to the old engine's scale. On the new one it would have marked every reading as fully trustworthy.

Fixed

Crops were the wrong shape

Each crop was squared off, which tripled its width for an upright person and pulled the neighbouring player fully into frame. The subject ended up filling a fifth of the picture.

Fixed

The app could never say "I do not know"

Subject selection always returned the nearest body, with no distance limit, so a tap anywhere would select someone. It would analyse a player on the next court rather than admit it lost you.

Fixed

Ball tracking was about to break

Sharing one detector between people and the ball meant a player would outscore the ball on every frame. Caught while building the fix, not after.

What is still unproven

These matter as much as the wins, and none of them are resolved.

Open

The accuracy score grades itself

"Did the reading land on the right body" is judged against the same box that produced it. That measures internal consistency, not identity. A box drawn on a spectator produces that spectator's reading and counts as correct. The true wrong person rate is unknown and is worse than 11%.

Open

A third of people are still missed

66% found means 34% not found. On wide court footage the engine finds nobody at all without cropping first.

Open

Nothing is device verified

Everything here is measured on extracted frames, not through the real app on a real phone. That check has still never been done, for this engine or the previous one.

Open

Two changes are tangled together

Crop shape and detector sensitivity changed at the same time, so their individual effects cannot be separated from this run alone.

Open

The deciding experiment has never run

The plan says measured biomechanics must beat a plain visual read in blind comparison, or the differentiator is not real. That comparison has never been run, not once. It is the next thing to do.

The deciding test, now run

Your plan has always contained a condition: measured biomechanics must beat a plain visual read in blind comparison, or the differentiator is not real. That test had never been run. It has now.

Thirteen clips. Both sides received identical frames, the identical rubric, the identical output specification, the identical model at the identical effort. The only difference was whether the measurement block was attached. Three judges per clip, shown the two answers without knowing which was which, in an order shuffled per clip.

The finding that matters most

A third of the measurements are impossible, and they carry confidence scores high enough to pass the honesty gate. The layer built to stop the product inventing things is itself the source of invented precision, and the check meant to catch that cannot see it, because the underlying engine reports near total confidence in everything it produces.

Every judge landed on the same point independently: hedging a fabricated number as "low confidence" does not repair it. The plain visual read wins because it declines to state what it cannot see.

The honest summary

The engine went from finding one player in twenty to finding two in three, and wrong body readings nearly halved. Two long dead measurements work again, one safety check that was about to fail silently now cannot, and a test suite that was flattering you now tells the truth. All of that is real.

And it does not matter as much as the last result. The measured path lost every clip on every axis to simply showing the frames to the model, while costing more, because a third of what it measures is impossible and it reports those figures confidently. The engineering worked. The premise underneath it did not survive contact with the evidence.

That is a good outcome for one night. The alternative was finding out after launch, from a coach who noticed the app told an athlete they jumped seven centimetres.

What this suggests

Not a decision, a reading of the evidence. Three routes, in order of how much they ask of you.

Recommended

Keep the detector, drop the measurements

Person detection is the part that demonstrably works, and it is what makes tapping a player possible. Keep it purely to find and mark the athlete, then let the vision model do the assessment. You lose the fifteen megabyte download, the on-device compute, and the invented numbers, and you keep reliable subject selection.

Slower

Fix the measurements first, then retest

The numbers are not wrong by a little. Fixing them means calibrated confidence, validated joint positions, and a real ground truth to check against, which is weeks of work with no guarantee. Only worth it if measured biomechanics is genuinely the business you want to be in.

Cheapest

Ship vision only

Fewest moving parts, lowest cost per analysis, no wrong body risk. Costs you the differentiator as currently written, so the positioning would need rewriting around coaching quality and progression instead of measurement.
